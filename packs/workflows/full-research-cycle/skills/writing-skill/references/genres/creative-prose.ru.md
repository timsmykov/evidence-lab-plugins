# Creative Prose

Use for fiction, scenes, literary fragments, dialogue, narrative essays with scene work, monologues, stylized prose, character sketches, and prose revision.

## Core Goal

Write prose that feels authored, situated, and alive. The scene or fragment should have point of view, pressure, specificity, and rhythm. Do not produce generic literary smoothness, stock emotion, or exposition disguised as scene.

## Brief Fields

Resolve:

- language
- form: scene, story, dialogue, fragment, monologue, vignette, narrative essay
- genre: literary, thriller, romance, fantasy, sci-fi, YA, autofiction, etc.
- POV: first, close third, omniscient, second person, rotating POV
- tense
- characters and relationships
- setting and time period
- scene purpose: what changes by the end
- desire, conflict, obstacle, or pressure
- emotional target
- style sample or desired style
- constraints: length, rating, canon facts, taboo content, continuity
- whether to preserve roughness or polish heavily

## Core Craft Rules

- Start close to a moment, not with generic setup.
- Keep POV disciplined: the narration can know only what the chosen POV permits.
- Let characters reveal themselves through action, choice, speech, omission, and attention.
- Use sensory detail selectively; one specific detail beats five generic ones.
- Dialogue should carry subtext, not only information.
- Interiority should be specific, not a label.
- Preserve authorial voice; do not make every sentence equally polished.
- Stop when the scene's work is done.

## Common LLM Fiction Failure Modes

### CP-1. Telling instead of dramatizing

Weak patterns:

- `она почувствовала грусть`
- `he felt a wave of sadness`
- `she was angry and disappointed`
- explaining the emotion after action already shows it

Fix: show the emotion through perception, behavior, choice, rhythm, or what the character notices.

Example:

- Weak: `She felt lonely.`
- Better: `She set out two cups, then put one back without looking at it.`

### CP-2. Generic sensory wallpaper

Weak details:

- `gentle breeze`
- `blooming flowers`
- `golden sunlight`
- `тёплый ветерок`
- `тишина обволакивала комнату`
- `сердце сжалось`

Fix: choose details that reveal character, setting, conflict, or change. Sensory detail should do narrative work.

### CP-3. Stock body language

Watch for:

- clenched fists
- shaky breath
- tight jaw
- averted gaze
- racing heart
- tears welling up
- `она опустила глаза`
- `он тяжело вздохнул`

Fix: use body language only when it is character-specific or changes the beat. Otherwise replace with action, silence, evasion, or choice.

### CP-4. Flat character voice

AI often gives every character the same articulate, emotionally literate voice.

Fix:

- give each character a pressure, not just a vocabulary
- vary sentence length, evasiveness, directness, humor, education, class, age, and relationship power
- let characters misunderstand or avoid the point
- preserve dialect/register only when requested and respectful

### CP-5. On-the-nose dialogue

Weak dialogue explains exactly what everyone means:

- `I am angry because you betrayed me.`
- `Ты меня обидел, потому что нарушил обещание.`

Fix: make dialogue do more than one thing: hide, test, attack, comfort, stall, seduce, deflect, negotiate, or reveal status.

Example:

- Weak: `I want a divorce.` / `You want a divorce? That hurts me.`
- Better: `I left the papers by the kettle.` / `You always did like an audience for breakfast.`

### CP-6. Predictable scene arc

AI scenes often move too cleanly: setup, conflict, confession, resolution.

Fix:

- keep tension unresolved when useful
- add a complication or reversal
- let characters make choices with cost
- avoid solving emotional conflict immediately

### CP-7. Exposition during peak moments

AI often pauses a fight, kiss, discovery, or argument to explain backstory.

Fix: move exposition earlier/later, embed it in conflict, or cut it. During pressure, characters notice what matters now.

### CP-8. Generic literary metaphors

Weak patterns:

- memories as shadows
- silence as a blanket
- heart as shattered glass
- city as a living organism
- grief as an ocean

Fix: use image systems tied to character, place, profession, memory, or motif.

### CP-9. POV leaks

Symptoms:

- the narration names another character's hidden emotion in close POV
- a character observes details they cannot know
- tone shifts from character perception to omniscient explanation

Fix: restrict perception. Use external signs or the POV character's interpretation.

### CP-10. Over-polishing and voice erasure

AI often smooths rough voice into generic literary competence.

Fix:

- preserve fragments, repetition, abruptness, awkwardness, or plainness when they belong to the voice
- do not synonym-cycle distinctive words
- do not make every sentence lyrical

## Scene Drafting Shape

Use this as a diagnostic, not a rigid template:

1. **Entry in motion** — begin close to a moment, choice, line of dialogue, image, or disruption.
2. **Orientation through action** — reveal setting and situation through what characters do.
3. **Pressure or desire** — someone wants something, avoids something, hides something, or resists something.
4. **Exchange / obstacle / discovery** — the scene creates friction.
5. **Shift** — knowledge, relationship, power, mood, stakes, or self-understanding changes.
6. **Exit** — leave with momentum, resonance, cost, or unresolved tension.

If nothing changes, the scene is probably a description, not a scene.

## Dialogue

- Give each speaker a different pressure.
- Let people evade, compress, interrupt, misunderstand, or answer the thing they wish had been asked.
- Use silence as action when it changes the beat.
- Use `said` freely in English and simple tags in Russian.
- Use action beats only when they reveal timing, tension, or choice.
- Avoid dialogue where characters summarize the plot for the reader.
- Do not overuse names in direct address.

## Interiority

Interiority should be specific and embodied, not abstract commentary.

Weak:

- `He realized he had always been afraid of intimacy.`

Stronger:

- `He counted the exits before he answered her.`

Use direct reflection when the genre and voice support it, but make it sound like this character thinking, not the narrator explaining therapy notes.

## Description

Description should earn its space by doing at least one job:

- locate the reader
- reveal character attention
- create mood
- foreshadow conflict
- mark social/class/historical context
- intensify pressure
- contrast with action

Avoid static paragraphs of visual inventory unless the style explicitly calls for it.

## Russian Creative Notes

- Avoid pseudo-literary fog: `тень воспоминаний`, `тишина обволакивала`, `сердце сжалось`, `душа наполнилась` unless earned.
- Russian can carry rhythm through syntax; do not overdecorate every noun.
- Let speech be less polished than narration when character demands it.
- Russian dialogue may be elliptical, abrupt, or syntactically uneven; preserve that if it fits.
- Psychological reflection can be richer than in minimalist English prose, but it still needs pressure and specificity.

## English Creative Notes

- Avoid generic MFA smoothness.
- Let sentence rhythm match POV and moment.
- Prefer concrete image over abstract emotional summary.
- English dialogue often benefits from compression, subtext, and implication.
- Use contractions in dialogue when natural for the character.

## Revision Modes

### Generate from scratch

- Ask for missing core constraints only if they materially affect the result: POV, genre, scene purpose, characters.
- If enough is known, proceed with assumptions and preserve room for revision.

### Rewrite / improve existing prose

- Preserve plot facts, POV, tense, character intention, and authorial voice.
- Identify the main problem first: generic detail, exposition, flat dialogue, POV leak, rhythm, scene shape.
- Do not rewrite into a different style unless requested.

### Continue a fragment

- Infer voice from the fragment.
- Preserve sentence rhythm, diction, POV distance, tense, and level of explicitness.
- Continue the pressure already present rather than introducing unrelated drama.

## Rewrite Examples

| Weak / AI-like | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Она была очень грустна. За окном шёл дождь.` | `Она поставила вторую чашку на стол и только потом вспомнила, что больше некому наливать.` | Shows grief through action. |
| `A gentle breeze moved through the blooming flowers.` | `The tulips knocked against the porch rail, their heads too heavy after the rain.` | Specific image instead of wallpaper. |
| `He felt a wave of anger wash over him.` | `He folded the receipt once, twice, until the paper split at the crease.` | Behavior carries emotion. |
| `Я злюсь, потому что ты меня предал.` | `— Ты всё-таки сказал ему. — Она не спросила; просто поставила ключи на край стола.` | Subtext and action beat. |
| `The silence wrapped around them like a blanket.` | `The refrigerator clicked off. After that, even his sleeve against the chair sounded too loud.` | Concrete sound creates silence. |
| `Он был храбрым и решительным человеком.` | `Он боялся так сильно, что говорил почти шёпотом, но всё равно не отошёл от двери.` | Character revealed through contradiction. |

## Quality Check

Before finalizing:

1. What changes in the scene or fragment?
2. Does POV leak?
3. Does each character sound distinct?
4. Are emotions dramatized where it matters?
5. Is any exposition placed where it breaks pressure?
6. Did the draft preserve ambiguity where ambiguity is useful?
7. Are sensory details specific and narratively useful?
8. Does dialogue carry subtext rather than only information?
9. Did revision preserve the author's voice rather than over-polishing it?
10. Are stock gestures, generic metaphors, and therapy-note explanations removed?
