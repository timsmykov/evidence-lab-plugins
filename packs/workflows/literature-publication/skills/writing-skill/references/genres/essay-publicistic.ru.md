# Essay and Publicistic Writing

Use for columns, blog posts, opinion essays, analytical posts, reflective essays, newsletters, cultural criticism, public-facing explainers, and narrative argument pieces.

## Core Goal

Write a piece with a real line of thought, a visible authorial stance, and concrete movement from beginning to end. Do not flatten the author's voice into a neutral school-essay overview. Do not create false balance when the genre calls for judgment.

## Brief Fields

Resolve:

- thesis, central question, or unresolved tension
- intended reader
- publication context or platform
- desired stance: explanatory, argumentative, reflective, polemical, calm, ironic, intimate, analytical
- degree of authorial presence: first person, restrained observer, expert voice, narrator, columnist
- source material, examples, anecdotes, or evidence
- claims that require support
- length and format
- language and voice sample
- forbidden tones, phrases, or positions

## Genre Subtypes

### Literary / reflective essay

Use when the piece explores an idea through memory, observation, association, scene, or personal pressure.

- Let the logic be felt, not only stated.
- Preserve ambiguity where it is productive.
- Use concrete images, moments, and turns of thought.
- Avoid forcing every paragraph into thesis-proof-conclusion shape.

### Analytical essay / explainer

Use when the reader needs to understand a topic, mechanism, trend, conflict, or decision.

- State the question early.
- Define terms only when needed.
- Move from context to mechanism to implication.
- Use examples as evidence, not decoration.

### Opinion column / publicistic article

Use when the piece argues for a position.

- Take a reasoned stance.
- Avoid presenting every side as equally plausible if the evidence does not support that.
- Address the strongest counterpoint, not a strawman.
- End with a consequence, decision, or sharper question.

### Blog / newsletter

Use when the piece is personal, direct, useful, or conversational.

- Start from a concrete problem, observation, or reader situation.
- Keep structure visible but not bureaucratic.
- Use first person and direct address only if the voice supports it.
- Avoid generic thought-leadership tone.

## Common LLM Failure Modes

### EP-1. Flattened voice

AI tends to average the voice into safe, polite neutrality. It removes pressure, idiosyncrasy, angle, and risk.

Fix:

- preserve the author's stance and preferred rhythm
- keep a few distinctive words or sentence shapes from the sample
- avoid replacing sharp phrasing with generic balance
- do not neutralize justified criticism

### EP-2. False balance

Weak pattern: presenting two sides with equal weight even when the task asks for a judgment or the evidence points one way.

Fix:

- name the stronger position
- explain why the weaker position is limited
- use counterargument as a turn, not as a substitute for thinking

### EP-3. Mechanical essay structure

Weak patterns:

- `В заключение можно сказать...`
- `In conclusion, it is clear that...`
- `Overall...`
- paragraph sequence that merely restates the outline
- predictable intro -> three points -> generic conclusion

Fix:

- start with tension, scene, claim, or problem
- make each section add a new move
- use a turn or complication before the ending
- end on a concrete implication, not a summary slogan

### EP-4. Motivational or LinkedIn-style closure

Avoid endings that sound uplifting but say nothing:

- `Будущее уже наступило.`
- `Именно поэтому важно начать уже сегодня.`
- `The future belongs to those who adapt.`
- `This is just the beginning.`

Fix: end on a decision, limit, consequence, observation, image, or unresolved question.

### EP-5. Unsupported broad claims

Do not write:

- `исследования показывают`
- `эксперты считают`
- `society is moving toward...`
- `everyone now understands...`

unless a source, argument, or clear context is provided.

Fix: cite, mark placeholder, narrow the claim, or frame it as the author's observation.

### EP-6. Generic examples

AI often uses examples that could fit any topic.

Fix:

- use examples supplied by the user
- if no example exists, use a clearly hypothetical example or ask for source material
- avoid fake anecdotes and invented scenes in factual essays

### EP-7. Over-smoothing

The model may make every sentence similarly polished and every transition explicit.

Fix:

- allow rhythm variation
- keep short sentences where they carry force
- remove transition phrases when adjacency is enough
- preserve productive roughness from the author's voice

## Structure

Use a visible but not mechanical line of thought:

1. **Entry** — concrete scene, tension, question, or claim.
2. **Context** — enough for the reader to understand why this matters.
3. **Thesis / pressure** — what the piece is really saying or trying to resolve.
4. **Development** — 2-4 moves, each adding something new: evidence, example, distinction, mechanism, consequence.
5. **Turn** — complication, counterpoint, reframing, contradiction, or limit.
6. **Ending** — concrete observation, decision, image, implication, or question.

Do not force every essay into this exact order. Use it as a diagnostic: if the piece has no turn, no pressure, or no real ending, revise.

## Russian Notes

- Avoid `в современном мире`, `в эпоху цифровизации`, `на сегодняшний день` openings.
- Avoid `с одной стороны / с другой стороны` unless the piece genuinely compares positions.
- Russian publicistic prose can allow evaluative language, irony, cultural memory, and sharper authorial pressure than a neutral English explainer.
- Do not confuse Russian publicistic voice with bureaucratic formality.
- Let some sentences be short.
- Replace broad abstractions with observed details.
- Do not overcorrect into casual slang unless the publication voice supports it.

## English Notes

- Avoid `In today's fast-paced world`, `Overall`, `It is clear that`, and generic thesis statements.
- English essays and blogs usually reward concision, explicit argument, and clean evidence.
- First person is acceptable in essays, newsletters, and opinion pieces when the author is part of the argument.
- Avoid false neutrality when the piece asks for judgment.
- Use contractions only when publication context and voice allow.

## Source and Evidence Policy

- Use provided facts, examples, quotations, and links first.
- Do not invent studies, statistics, public reactions, anecdotes, or named cases.
- If a factual claim needs support, insert `[источник]`, `[citation needed]`, or narrow the claim.
- Strong argumentation does not mean unsupported certainty. It means clear reasoning within known evidence.

## Rewrite Examples

| Weak / AI-like | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `В заключение можно сказать, что всем важно действовать осторожно.` | `Без чёткого плана осторожность превращается в паузу, а пауза — в потерянное время.` | Ends with a concrete consequence instead of generic summary. |
| `In conclusion, everyone should take action on this issue.` | `The harder question is not whether to act, but which step can be taken without pretending the tradeoffs are gone.` | Creates a real ending and avoids motivational closure. |
| `С одной стороны, технология полезна, с другой — она несёт риски.` | `Технология полезна там, где снимает ручную работу. Риск начинается в другом месте: когда ей передают право решать без проверки.` | Replaces false balance with distinction. |
| `This topic is important in today's world.` | `The topic matters because it changes who gets to decide, how fast, and with what evidence.` | Replaces universal opener with stakes. |
| `Многие эксперты считают, что такой подход эффективен.` | `Чтобы утверждать эффективность, нужны данные: кто применял подход, в каких условиях и что изменилось.` | Removes fake authority. |

## Quality Check

Before finalizing:

1. Does the piece have a real point of view or central pressure?
2. Does each section move the argument forward?
3. Are examples concrete and sourced/provided?
4. Is there a turn, complication, or limit?
5. Does the ending avoid a motivational one-liner?
6. Did the draft preserve the author's voice rather than flattening it?
7. Are unsupported factual claims removed, narrowed, or marked?
8. Is the tone appropriate for Russian or English conventions rather than a translated hybrid?
