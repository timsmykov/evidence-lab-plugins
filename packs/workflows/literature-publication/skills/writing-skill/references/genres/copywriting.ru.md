# Copywriting

Use for landing pages, product copy, ads, email campaigns, social posts, onboarding screens, app text, offers, CTAs, launch copy, product narratives, and persuasive commercial writing.

## Core Goal

Write persuasive copy from provided facts only. The copy should make the reader understand the offer, believe the claim, and know what to do next. It must not rely on generic hype, fake urgency, invented social proof, or unsupported transformation promises.

## Source Discipline

Never invent proof.

Do not fabricate:

- customer numbers
- conversion lifts
- testimonials
- reviews
- case studies
- certifications
- awards
- guarantees
- legal, medical, financial, or compliance claims
- product capabilities
- scarcity, urgency, deadlines, or price claims
- competitor comparisons

If proof is missing, write around it, soften the claim, or mark `[proof needed]` / `[нужно подтверждение]`.

Bad: `Join thousands of happy customers` when no customer count was provided.

Better: `Designed for teams that need [specific use case].`

## Brief Fields

Resolve:

- product/service
- audience and situation
- pain, desire, job-to-be-done, or trigger moment
- offer and scope
- proof available: metrics, testimonials, demos, case studies, awards, certifications, screenshots, product facts
- objections and risks
- desired action
- channel: landing, email, ad, UI, social, script
- funnel stage: awareness, consideration, conversion, activation, retention, winback
- tone and brand voice
- language and locale
- regulated-industry constraints

## Core Structure

Not every text needs every part, but know the parts:

- Audience situation.
- Problem or friction.
- Promise or useful outcome.
- Mechanism: how it works.
- Proof: provided facts only.
- Objection handling.
- CTA.

The strongest copy usually moves from concrete reader situation to believable mechanism to proof-backed action.

## Common LLM Copywriting Failure Modes

### CW-1. Generic transformation hype

Weak patterns:

- `transform your business`
- `unlock your potential`
- `elevate your workflow`
- `take your X to the next level`
- `revolutionize the way you...`
- `boost productivity like never before`

Fix: name the actual outcome, mechanism, or friction removed.

Example:

- Weak: `Transform your team's productivity.`
- Better: `See overdue tasks, blockers, and owners in one view before the weekly meeting.`

### CW-2. Fake urgency and scarcity

Weak patterns:

- `limited time offer`
- `act now`
- `don't miss out`
- `spots are filling fast`
- `only a few seats left`

Fix: use urgency only if the user provided a real deadline, capacity limit, event date, or price change.

If urgency is not real, create momentum through relevance: why the reader should care now based on their situation.

### CW-3. Unsupported social proof

Do not invent:

- `trusted by leading companies`
- `loved by thousands`
- `customers report...`
- fake testimonials
- fake star ratings
- fake logos

Fix: use provided proof, or omit proof. If proof exists but is vague, make it precise only within provided facts.

### CW-4. Empty adjectives and superlatives

Weak words:

- English: `seamless`, `powerful`, `robust`, `innovative`, `best-in-class`, `state-of-the-art`, `world-class`
- Russian: `уникальный`, `инновационный`, `эффективный`, `комплексный`, `премиальный`, `лидирующий`, `многоступенчатый`, `эксклюзивный`

Fix: replace adjective with capability, proof, constraint, or result.

Example:

- Weak: `a powerful, seamless platform`
- Better: `a workspace where managers assign tasks, track blockers, and export weekly reports`

### CW-5. Formula misuse

PAS, AIDA, BAB, and similar frameworks are useful planning tools, not visible templates.

Failure signs:

- exaggerated pain
- obvious agitation
- generic benefit stacking
- CTA that does not match the reader's readiness

Fix: use the framework invisibly. The final copy should sound like a human brand speaking to a real buyer, not a template filled with variables.

### CW-6. Benefit without mechanism

Weak: `Save time and grow faster.`

Better: `Import invoices once, auto-match them to payments, and flag missing documents before month-end.`

Rule: every major benefit needs either a mechanism, proof, or clear use case.

### CW-7. Tone inconsistency

AI copy often jumps between premium, friendly, technical, urgent, and inspirational.

Fix:

- choose one voice per asset
- adapt by channel: ad, landing page, email, onboarding, social post
- preserve brand vocabulary if provided
- remove phrases that belong to a different audience or price point

### CW-8. Unsafe or regulated claims

Be extra conservative with:

- finance
- medicine / health
- legal services
- education outcomes
- employment / income claims
- supplements, wellness, or safety claims
- guarantees and warranties

Fix: use factual descriptions, eligibility language, disclaimers if provided, and placeholders where legal approval is needed.

## Channel Patterns

### Landing section

- Headline: concrete outcome or sharp positioning.
- Subhead: who it is for, what it does, why it matters.
- 2-4 proof-backed bullets or mechanism bullets.
- CTA: one action.

Avoid: stuffing every benefit into the hero. The hero should orient and motivate, not close every objection.

### Email campaign

- Subject: specific, not clickbait.
- First line: tied to reader situation or reason for writing.
- Body: one idea per email.
- Proof or mechanism: one strong support point.
- CTA: low-friction next step.

Avoid: generic greetings, fake urgency, multi-topic emails.

### Ad

- Hook: concrete pain, desire, contrast, or offer.
- Body: one reason to believe.
- CTA: action matching funnel stage.

Avoid: vague `Looking for a better way to...` unless the brand voice supports direct-response cliché.

### Product / UI copy

- Tell the user what happened, what it means, and what to do next.
- Use plain verbs.
- Keep labels short but not cryptic.
- Error messages should help recovery.

Avoid: cleverness that slows action.

### Social post

- One idea.
- Strong opening line.
- Concrete example or point of view.
- Natural close.

Avoid: LinkedIn-style moralizing and tidy inspirational endings.

## Audience / Market Notes

### B2B SaaS

Prefer:

- workflow friction
- decision criteria
- integration and implementation facts
- security/compliance facts if provided
- proof from teams, time saved, risk reduced, or process visibility

Avoid: vague `drive value` and `scale your business` claims.

### Consumer product

Prefer:

- sensory specificity
- use occasion
- emotional benefit tied to real product features
- simple proof

Avoid: inflated lifestyle promises.

### Expert service

Prefer:

- problem diagnosis
- process clarity
- credibility proof
- scope and fit

Avoid: guru tone, guaranteed outcomes, fake scarcity.

### Education

Prefer:

- learner situation
- curriculum, practice, feedback, outcomes if proven
- realistic expectations

Avoid: guaranteed career or income claims unless legally approved and sourced.

### Regulated industries

Prefer:

- exact product/service facts
- compliant terminology
- disclaimers or approval placeholders

Avoid: unsupported claims about health, wealth, safety, legality, or guaranteed results.

## Russian Copy Notes

- Avoid empty adjectives: `уникальный`, `инновационный`, `эффективный`, `комплексный`, `премиальный`, `лидирующий` without proof.
- Avoid bureaucratic marketing: `оказываем услуги по реализации`, `осуществляем внедрение решений`.
- Avoid direct English calques and pseudo-premium tone.
- Prefer concrete user outcomes, native Russian idiom, and clear mechanism.
- Keep CTAs natural: `Оставить заявку`, `Записаться`, `Получить расчёт`, `Посмотреть демо`.
- Do not use fake urgency if no real deadline exists.

Example:

- Weak: `Уникальный продукт обеспечит вам лидерство на рынке.`
- Better: `Сервис автоматически распределяет заявки между менеджерами и показывает, где клиент ждёт ответа дольше нормы.`

## English Copy Notes

- Avoid `seamless`, `robust`, `unlock`, `supercharge`, `elevate`, `drive value` unless brand voice supports them or they are technically precise.
- Avoid vague transformation claims.
- Use clear benefit + mechanism + friction reducer.
- Use `you` when the channel and brand voice support direct address.
- Let proof do the persuasion.

Example:

- Weak: `Leverage our state-of-the-art platform to unlock growth.`
- Better: `Track every lead from first call to signed contract without updating three spreadsheets.`

## CTA Rules

A CTA should match the reader's readiness.

- Awareness: `Learn more`, `Read the guide`, `See how it works`.
- Consideration: `Compare plans`, `Watch demo`, `Calculate cost`.
- Conversion: `Start trial`, `Book a demo`, `Get a quote`, `Оставить заявку`.
- Activation: `Create your first project`, `Import contacts`, `Invite your team`.

Avoid generic CTAs when a more specific action is available.

## Rewrite Examples

| Weak / AI-like | Stronger | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Transform your business with our innovative solution.` | `Replace manual status chasing with one dashboard for tasks, blockers, and owners.` | Mechanism instead of hype. |
| `Limited time offer — act now!` | `Registration closes Friday because the workshop is capped at 25 participants.` | Uses urgency only if real. |
| `Trusted by thousands of happy customers.` | `Used by [customer segment] to [provided use case].` | Avoids fake proof. |
| `Уникальный продукт для эффективной работы.` | `Сервис собирает заявки, распределяет их по менеджерам и показывает просроченные ответы.` | Replaces empty adjectives. |
| `100% guaranteed result.` | `[нужно юридическое подтверждение гарантии]` or `Мы покажем, какие результаты можно ожидать на основе [данные].` | Avoids unsafe guarantee. |
| `Looking for a better way to manage your team?` | `When every update lives in a different chat, planning turns into detective work.` | Stronger reader situation. |

## Quality Check

Before finalizing:

1. Is every claim believable from provided facts?
2. Are proof, numbers, testimonials, urgency, and guarantees real or clearly marked as missing?
3. Is the CTA specific and appropriate to the funnel stage?
4. Does the copy sound like a real brand, not a template?
5. Are unsupported superlatives removed?
6. Is there one clear action?
7. Does every major benefit have a mechanism or proof?
8. Is the voice consistent across the asset?
9. Are RU/EN copy conventions handled separately?
10. Are regulated claims treated conservatively?
