# Academic and Research Writing Mode

Use this reference when the requested output is a manuscript section, research report, proposal, academic essay, literature synthesis, method description, results narrative, discussion, abstract, or response to comments.

## Evidence model

Keep three layers distinct:

1. **Source-backed statement** — traceable to a real source or supplied dataset.
2. **Author interpretation** — a bounded inference whose assumptions are visible.
3. **Open question or limitation** — evidence is absent, conflicting, inaccessible, or outside the search boundary.

Never convert one layer into another during rewriting. A source summary is not automatically an accepted fact; an interpretation is not a result; an unlocated source is not evidence that no source exists.

## Before drafting

Confirm or infer only when low risk:

- document type and intended venue;
- audience and disciplinary register;
- research question or argument;
- allowed evidence set and citation style;
- section function and length;
- author voice and disclosure requirements.

If the task would require choosing a thesis, evidence boundary, interpretation, or substantive conclusion not supplied by the researcher, stop at a compact proposal and obtain confirmation.

## Section functions

- **Introduction:** establish the bounded problem, significance supported by evidence, gap, and research question.
- **Literature synthesis:** compare findings, methods, populations, assumptions, and limitations; do not produce an annotated list.
- **Methods:** report what was actually done with enough detail for assessment or reproduction; do not silently improve the method in prose.
- **Results:** describe analyses and uncertainty without causal or evaluative inflation.
- **Discussion:** interpret within the design and evidence, compare with sources, state limitations, and separate implications from findings.
- **Conclusion:** answer only what the study supports; do not introduce new evidence or universal claims.
- **Abstract:** compress the actual question, method, principal results, and bounded conclusion without adding claims absent from the body.

## Claim discipline

- Map factual, numerical, historical, methodological, and literature-dependent claims to real source identifiers.
- Preserve effect direction, magnitude, uncertainty, population, conditions, and time boundaries.
- Use causal language only when design and assumptions justify it.
- Keep core terms stable; do not synonym-cycle technical constructs.
- State searches as bounded procedures: use "not found within the documented search" rather than "no studies exist."
- Use placeholders instead of invented authors, titles, journals, years, DOIs, page numbers, sample sizes, or quotations.

## Revision checks

1. Does each paragraph perform a clear function?
2. Are sourced claims, interpretation, and limitations distinguishable?
3. Did revision alter numbers, conditions, citations, or evidential strength?
4. Are uncertainty and competing explanations preserved?
5. Does the conclusion stay within the design and reported evidence?
6. Is accountable human authorship and approval still explicit?

## Boundaries

This mode supports accountable researchers in drafting and revision. It must not conceal AI use, fabricate scholarship, invent results, write qualification work on behalf of its author, bypass institutional rules, or imply acceptance by a journal or reviewer.
